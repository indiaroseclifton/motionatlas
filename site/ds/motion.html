Motion is routed.

Nothing in 01 floats, bounces or fades for decoration. Things travel along a trace, lock into place, resolve from 0s and 1s, and count in visible steps. Fast in, hard stop. Under reduced motion every one of these becomes instant.

The four moves

Every animation in the system is one of these, or two of them in sequence. Press replay to see each with the real tokens.

01 · Route

Arrive along a trace, never from nowhere. The line draws first with --e-route, then the element follows it and locks.

02 · Lock

The house ease, cubic-bezier(.2,.9,.1,1): almost all of the travel happens at once, then it settles hard. Used for anything arriving.

LOCKED

03 · Decode

Headlines resolve left to right from 0s and 1s, 14ms per character with eight characters of noise ahead. Once, on arrival, never on text being read.

04 · Tick

Numbers count in visible steps with steps(6) and a tick sound when sound is on. Never a smooth tween: people should see what changed.

Reduced motion

When a person asks their system for less motion, 01 gives them none. 01.css turns off every animation and transition; 01.js resolves decodes instantly; City3D renders a single still frame; ticking values jump straight to their final number.

  • Animations and transitions are disabled globally by the reduced-motion block in 01.css.
  • Decode sets the final text immediately (O1.reduce is true).
  • Live dots, cursors and skeletons stop pulsing but keep their colour, so state is still readable.
  • Looping motion anywhere in view, such as the city or a marquee, gets a pause control even when motion is allowed.

Do and don't

Do

  • Decode headlines and new agent names once, on arrival.
  • Tick counters in six steps, with the tick sound only when sound is on.
  • Keep product motion at or under 320ms, and never block input.
  • Make every effect instant under reduced motion.

Don't

  • Decode body text, labels or anything someone is reading.
  • Bounce, spring, wobble or overshoot.
  • Animate layout while a person is typing or choosing.
  • Loop motion in peripheral vision without a pause control.
