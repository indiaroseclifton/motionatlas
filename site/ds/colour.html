Graphite, platinum, a little light.

Graphite carries almost everything. Platinum is for type. Cyan is the signal, and there is only ever one. Green means live. Purple and amber are rare enough that people notice when they appear.

Graphite

Seven steps from near-black to slate. The page is 950; surfaces step up one at a time. Never skip more than two steps between neighbours, and never use pure black.

Platinum

Three greys for type. Platinum for headings and anything you must read, platinum 2 for body copy, platinum 3 for labels and metadata. All three pass AA on every graphite surface up to 800.

Signal and accents

Cyan is the system's voice: focus, the active route, the one action that matters. Cyan white is its hover. Cyan deep is the only cyan allowed on light grounds. Green is reserved for live, healthy and done. Amber warns, red faults, purple marks the experimental.

Semantic roles

Write roles in your CSS, not raw values. Roles are how Workspace and the showcase stay one system: when a surface changes, the role moves and every component follows.

Pairings that always work

Balance

On any screen, graphite should cover roughly three quarters of the pixels. If cyan creeps past a tenth, something that is not the signal is wearing its colour.

  • Platinum 17

Do and don't

Do

  • Use cyan for focus, the active route and the single primary signal on a screen.
  • Use green only for live, healthy or complete.
  • Keep amber for warnings and the occasional accent in data.
  • Pair every status colour with a label or an icon.
  • Use cyan deep (#0A7F94) whenever cyan has to sit on platinum or white.

Don't

  • Set body text in cyan, or fill large areas with it.
  • Use green for buttons, links or decoration.
  • Put purple and amber on the same screen as decoration.
  • Use pure black (#000) or pure white (#FFF) anywhere.
  • Introduce gradients between signal colours; gradients only fade a colour into graphite.
