City circuits.

01ai's one illustration: a motherboard drawn as a city at night. Chips are towers, traces are streets, light is traffic, and the camera is always angled and off-centre, as if you were flying in low over the downtown core.

  • Engines City3D · Hero2D
  • Camera angled, off-centre
  • Never overhead or symmetric

The metaphor

Chipsare towers.Tall near the core, low at the edges. The tallest few carry the green LEDs.
Tracesare streets.Right-angle routes in cyan, with trunk lines that carry most of the light.
Datais traffic.Points of cyan-white light moving along the traces; 6% green, rare purple and amber.

Live renders

These are the real engines from the direction kit, running here. City3D draws the board in three.js; Hero2D draws lighter 2D versions on a plain canvas for pages that cannot afford WebGL.

The camera

The single most important rule. A view straight down reads as a circuit diagram; a symmetric view reads as a screensaver. Angled and off-centre reads as a place you could fly into.

// camera travels from a to b as the page scrolls, looking from la to lb
City3D.start(canvas, {seed: 7, mode: 'solid', cam: {a: [34,15,40], b: [16,7,6], la: [-6,0,-14], lb: [-26,1,-46]}});

Do and don't

Do

  • Keep the camera low, angled and off-centre, with the horizon in the top third.
  • Leave the left of the frame quiet so a headline can sit on it.
  • Give looping renders a pause control, and show a still frame under reduced motion.
  • Fall back to city-poster.jpg when WebGL is missing.

Don't

  • Show the board from overhead or dead centre.
  • Mirror the skyline, or build a symmetric composition.
  • Use DNA helixes, brains, robots or glowing orbs as hero art.
  • Oversize secondary motifs, such as the DNA strand on Agents, past a background role.
