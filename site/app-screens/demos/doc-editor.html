Writing for customers

Four habits that make our letters, emails and screens easier to read. Read it once, then keep it open while you write.

Start with what the reader needs

Most people open a message with one question in mind: what do I have to do? Answer that in the first two lines. The background can follow, and often it can go.

Use the words they use

We say ‘bill’, not ‘statement of account’. We say ‘sorry’, not ‘we apologise for any inconvenience caused’. If a twelve-year-old would stumble on a word, find another.

If you would not say it across a counter, do not write it in an email.

Numbers, dates and money

  • Write dates as 12 November 2026, with no ‘th’.
  • Use numerals for anything you can count: 3 days, 14 lids, £20.
  • Put the currency sign first and leave out ‘.00’ unless the pennies matter.

Before you send

Read it aloud. Cut the first sentence if the second works without it. Then check the name, the date and the amount, because those are the three things people notice when they are wrong.
